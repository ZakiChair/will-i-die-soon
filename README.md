# Will I Die Soon? — Health Risk Explorer

> **Private research prototype.** This experience is not clinically validated, does not diagnose or predict death, and is not a public wellness or regulated medical release. Do not treat its qualitative outputs or Purity Score as medical advice, a disease probability, life expectancy, or a substitute for care.

An English-default, fully bilingual English/French health-risk exploration that keeps the journey in browser memory. A light landing presents a stylized 3D human silhouette surrounded by four health axes; the questionnaire and results report make the questions, points of support, evidence and next actions easier to read.

## Implemented scope

- **137 curated, fully structured questions** across 39 health domains, with stable IDs, deterministic eligibility, age gates, and answer-driven branches. The 2026-09-29 rewrite removed every free-text question and every question that no displayed computation read (58 removals, including the typed `lab_value_*` fields now superseded by the structured lab import), and added 19 structured items required by published screening instruments or by sourced red flags (exertional chest pain, exertional leg pain, irregular palpitations, cancer alarm signs). A second pass the same day removed 78 further context-only questions that no risk rule, screening instrument, Purity Score input, Express component, branch gate or displayed view read, together with the five domains they left empty (dental health, over-the-counter medicines, reproductive health, vaccinations, work exposures). Every remaining question feeds at least one displayed output; the social-support, balance-training, morning-light, mood-support and belonging items stay because the protective-roots overview displays them. The bank keeps former-smoking history; the walking-pace, stair, resting-heart-rate, grip, chair-rise and push-up items that only the unrendered longevity module read were removed in the second pass. The presentation separates a short question from useful criteria and explains common questions through their practical purpose.
- **Probable conditions from published instruments:** adult Quick/Detailed/Deep results add a "Conditions" section computed by the pure module `app/lib/pathology-risk.ts` from structured answers, the profile and confirmed lab values. Instruments: FINDRISC (type 2 diabetes, 0–26 points, published 10-year risk band), SCORE2 and SCORE2-OP (10-year fatal and non-fatal cardiovascular disease, ages 40–89, low-risk region calibration for Switzerland and the United Kingdom only, requires imported total and HDL cholesterol), STOP-Bang (obstructive sleep apnoea), AUDIT-C (hazardous drinking, sex-specific cut-offs), PHQ-2 (depression), GAD-2 (anxiety), COPD-PS (COPD, age 35+) and CAIDE (20-year dementia risk, ages 40–64). Each card states the status (complete, incomplete with the named missing inputs and the depth that asks them, or not applicable with the reason), points, category, the inputs used (derived inputs flagged) and sources. HbA1c, fasting glucose and eGFR are classified against ADA and KDIGO published ranges, and 14 Lancet 2024 modifiable dementia factors are listed as present, absent or unanswered. Percentages appear only under the private-research policy; every output is a screening estimate, not a diagnosis. Minors and Express never receive these scores.
- **Four ordered presentation pillars:** Cardio, VO₂ max & cellular energy / *Cardio, VO₂ max et énergie cellulaire*; Strength, nervous system & recovery / *Force, système nerveux et récupération*; Sleep & circadian rhythm / *Sommeil et rythme circadien*; Nutrition & metabolic health / *Alimentation et santé métabolique*. This is presentation grouping only.
- **Closed mapping guards:** tests require every current question (137), domain (39), and risk rule (59) to resolve exactly once; they also check 44 conditional questions, 54 gate edges, and the 16/23/2/18 risk-rule pillar distribution.
- **Express:** exactly 9 adult-only targeted questions, under 1 minute, covering reported VO₂ max, known squat/deadlift one-rep maxima, sleep, body context, and nutrition. It now returns a four-axis profile, an explicit fitness-and-habits index, up to three priorities, and the reported measurements. Seven interpretable components feed Cardio, Strength, Sleep and Nutrition; at least four components across two axes are required for a provisional index, and all seven for a complete one. Missing measurements stay missing. This separate product index is not a clinical risk estimate or population ranking; see [the complete Express method](docs/reviews/2026-09-07-express-profile.md).
- **Quick:** up to 20 eligible core questions, about 3 minutes; adults and adolescents reach the full 20, a guardian-assisted child under 13 currently has 17 eligible items, and the depth needs at least 10. For adults the 20 now cover the complete FINDRISC item set, PHQ-2, the four immediate red flags, blood-pressure and diagnosed-condition context, and the tobacco, alcohol and lab-import gates; adolescents swap waist and glucose history for pregnancy relevance and sexual-contact safety.
- **Detailed (medium-depth):** up to 50 eligible questions, about 8 minutes; adults and adolescents reach the full 50, a child under 13 currently has 42, and the depth needs at least 30. It adds the no-lab instrument items that Quick lacks (STOP-Bang, GAD-2, COPD-PS from age 35, CAIDE education and the SCORE2 prior-event exclusion). The queue keeps the 50 highest-priority eligible questions: below 35 it holds all 17 unconditional Purity Score inputs (the 8 conditional ones open as answers allow), while from 35 the three COPD-PS items displace sedentary hours, refreshing sleep and bedtime variation (14 of 17). The SCORE2 statin, family-history and inflammatory-condition modifiers, the diagnosed-high-cholesterol item, the recreational-substance gates, the dementia history items and the leg-pain and palpitation red flags are asked in Deep only.
- **Deep:** every relevant question. The queue takes all eligible unconditional questions plus the branches that answers open, capped at 200; currently 89 base items at age 18, 92 at 35 (the COPD-PS items open at 35) and 96 with the medication follow-up branch, for 15–20 minutes. Deep is reserved for adults (18+) with at least 80 eligible base questions; minors are offered Detailed instead of a silently shortened queue.
- **English + French:** English is the default. The complete interface, questions, qualitative results, score/action presentation, and exports localize to French without changing canonical IDs, values, units, rules, scores, or state. Locale is not persisted or placed in the URL.
- **Age and country routes:** the profile accepts whole ages 0–120 and requires Switzerland, United Kingdom, United States, or another country/region. Children under 13 require an assisting parent/guardian/trusted adult; adolescents 13–17 choose assisted or private answering; adults use the adult route. Age and country still filter question/rule applicability.
- **Local laboratory context:** manual entry is always available. Local import accepts plain-text `.txt`/`.text`, PDF, and image files; CSV and every other unsupported type fall back to manual entry. Extraction uses lazy PDF.js/Tesseract parsers and same-origin vendored worker assets. Draft rows require explicit review. Confirmed rows (`ConfirmedLabValue[]`) are now the only numeric lab source: the import no longer writes `lab_value_*` answers, and the same confirmed values feed SCORE2 (total and HDL cholesterol), CAIDE (total cholesterol) and the HbA1c/glucose/eGFR classifications, with unit conversion to the canonical mmol/L, mmol/mol and mL/min/1.73 m² handled in `app/lib/labs.ts`. Limits are 20 MiB, 50 PDF pages, and 500,000 extracted characters; the OCR input/output limits are guardrails, not hard peak-memory bounds for the decoded image and materialized OCR output.
- **Clearer questions and semantic validation:** known VO₂ max and maximal-lift measurements are explicitly optional; instructions to use existing results and not perform a new test are visible before the input. Applicable 0–10 scales have endpoint labels. Click and Enter share validation for missing or non-finite values, nonnegative quantities, positive height/weight, and unit-based bounds such as 0–24 sleep hours and 0–7 activity days per week. Errors are localized and announced; a deliberate skip remains available and stores `null`. These bounds are not medical reference ranges.
- **Explainable results:** immediate signals remain first. Adult Quick/Detailed/Deep reports then show support counts, topics to explore, weighted habits coverage and four descriptive pillar cards, then the "Conditions" screening section, followed by up to three prioritized actions, a theoretical score reference, the keyboard-accessible risk tree and evidence, the detailed score ledger, confirmed labs and export controls. Counts describe matched signals, not severity or domain scores. Express shows its own qualified profile and four scored axes: sleep and nutrition concerns retain priority even when fitness scores are high. Minors retain their distinct routes and private-results handoff. Anchors only link to available sections, and the normal results title receives focus on arrival.
- **Score references:** an eligible adult habits score is positioned on a 0–100 bell-curve axis, with its difference in points from a theoretical centre of 50. The centre and standard deviation of 15 are drawing conventions, not a measured cohort, population ranking, percentile or health threshold. The SVG has a text alternative; its HTML legend and ticks remain readable on mobile. Quick and insufficient-coverage results explain why no curve or position is shown. Express instead uses a four-axis radar with a dashed 75/100 product reference; missing axes are not drawn as zeroes or joined into a complete shape. This reference is not a population average. See the limits below.
- **Retained longevity module:** `app/lib/longevity.ts` and `app/components/longevity-synthesis.tsx` remain in the codebase, but `Results` no longer imports or renders that synthesis. Estimated age at death, cause-family weightings and year-gain claims are absent from the current results presentation.
- **Purity Score:** a transparent adult wellness-habit index for sufficiently covered Detailed/Deep assessments. It remains unavailable for Express; the separate Express index does not bypass that coverage gate or change this score. Quick shows a reflection rather than a number; under-18 or unverified-age routes do not receive an adult index. Age, sex, ethnicity, disability, diagnoses, family history, and unavoidable exposures cannot lower the Purity Score.
- **Explicit local handoff:** print and redacted JSON require clicks. The report schema is now `health-risk-explorer-report-v3`: adult Quick/Detailed/Deep reports add a `pathologyRisk` object (ruleset `pathology-scores-v1`, every instrument with its status, points, maximum points, category, level, SCORE2 modifiers, input identifiers (derived inputs flagged, answer values never written), missing inputs or not-applicable reason, source IDs, lab classifications, dementia factors, family-history context, dementia source IDs and the fixed interpretation `published-screening-instruments-not-a-diagnosis`); `riskPercent` and `riskHorizonYears` are present only when the policy allows them. Adult Express exports keep their derived `expressAssessment` object, including its `express-index-v1` method version, fixed references, source links and `referencePopulation: null`. Structured raw answers remain an adult-only opt-in; filenames, raw file/parser content, and private metadata are excluded, and the bank no longer contains free-text answers.
- **Human health portrait:** Sora headings and Source Sans 3 body/control text, with tabular measurement numerals and light landing, questionnaire and results surfaces. The bilingual landing centres a stylized human silhouette rendered with **Three.js 0.185.1**. The scroll story follows four actions: sleeping, running, deadlifting and eating. Four navigation links above the scene lead to Sleep, Cardio, Strength and Nutrition in that narrative order; questionnaire and result-axis ordering stay unchanged. On animated mobile views, a single visual copy of the active chapter sits below the character while the original chapter targets remain accessible. A preview explains the three report outputs—points of support, priorities and an index—without an example score. The body is a presentation of questionnaire topics, not validated anatomy or a measurement of the visitor's health. The shared visual wrapper loads the human scene on demand when visible and motion is allowed. Native-scroll transitions, viewport/hidden-tab pausing and the accessible pause/resume button are retained. A human SVG is present from the initial HTML and remains for reduced motion; a WebGL or decorative-render failure preserves the chapter story and immediate assessment routes. Existing media remains six local WebP presentation images and one silent local Cardio loop; these files do not power the silhouette. The Cardio video retains its poster until `canplay`, falls back to the poster on error, and is static/absent for reduced motion, hidden documents, and print. No runtime media is remote. Generated artwork was created with OpenAI's built-in image-generation tool and locally post-processed/encoded with local FFmpeg/cwebp tooling; it was not created with the Sora video model and contains no medical labels or factual diagrams. See [the current human-health landing review](docs/reviews/2026-09-07-human-health-home.md); the earlier [filament-sculpture review](docs/reviews/2026-09-07-living-atlas-home.md) is retained as history.

## Evidence limits

The private prototype evaluates 59 transparent qualitative rules across 14 declared groups (12 nonempty). It contains 29 authoritative-safety, 26 guideline-action, and 4 evidence-limited outputs, with **zero validated-estimate rules and zero clinical percentages inside the risk engine**. No hidden model or remote inference is used. Medication/substance outputs do not instruct starting, stopping, or changing a dose.

The separate pathology module (`app/lib/pathology-risk.ts`) is the only place that produces a percentage, and only by re-implementing published instruments with their published coefficients and cut-offs: FINDRISC (Lindström & Tuomilehto 2003), SCORE2 and SCORE2-OP (ESC 2021, low-risk region only, worked example 6.3% reproduced in tests), CAIDE model 1 (Kivipelto 2006), STOP-Bang, AUDIT-C, PHQ-2, GAD-2 and COPD-PS; lab bands follow the ADA 2025 Standards of Care and KDIGO 2024, and the dementia factor list follows the 2024 Lancet Commission. These are screening or risk-stratification tools validated in their own cohorts, not a diagnosis, not a validation of this product, and not calibrated for the United States or "other" country routes (SCORE2 reports `region-not-calibrated` there). `prototypePolicy.allowValidatedProbabilities` is `true` so percentages display; `publicWellnessPolicy` keeps it `false` and the module then returns points and category only. Missing inputs are named rather than imputed, "unsure"/"unknown" answers count as missing, and a single lab value never defines a disease. No instrument is computed for anyone under 18. The [design contract and verification record](docs/reviews/2026-09-29-pathologies-probables-design.md) list every input mapping, coefficient and test vector.

The Gaussian reference consumes the existing adult habits score without changing its arithmetic or eligibility. Detailed/Deep still require at least 70% weighted coverage, at least five answered categories, resolved tobacco/alcohol gates and nonzero assessed points. The bell curve uses `exp(-0.5 × ((score − 50) / 15)²)` as a drawing profile restricted to the score axis; it is not evidence that the bounded score follows a normal distribution. Its height and shaded area express no probability or individual risk. No participant cohort, percentile, health norm or predicted outcome is inferred. Comparing people would require a compatible observed dataset and separate validation; the current app neither collects one nor simulates one.

The Express index is an equally weighted mean of available axes, using unrounded component and axis values until the displayed result is rounded. Its VO₂ max and strength references are fixed product conventions, not norms adjusted for age, sex or measurement protocol. Sleep duration uses a 7–9-hour window before age 65 and 7–8 hours from age 65; the upper limit of 9 before age 61 is a product choice, while the CDC describes a minimum of 7 hours for ages 18–60. The sleep penalty, food-frequency points and profile thresholds are not clinically calibrated. General sleep and nutrition guidance does not validate these coefficients. A complete result can be favorable, mixed or require attention; missing components keep the profile incomplete unless a sleep/nutrition signal takes priority. The [Express method and examples](docs/reviews/2026-09-07-express-profile.md) detail every formula, threshold, missing-data rule and source.

The retained longevity helper (`app/lib/longevity.ts`) is a separate, unvalidated heuristic based on a simplified life table and year-denominated adjustments. It is no longer called by the results presentation, and its values are not used in the overview, pillar cards, reference curve or current export report. Keeping its source code is not an endorsement of its estimates.

See [the complete evidence register](docs/evidence-register.md) for every rule, source, population and consumption status, and [the privacy/release record](docs/privacy-and-release.md) for the data boundary, audits, response hardening, and exact private-deployment prerequisites.

## Privacy boundary

There is no account, application backend, database, analytics, session replay, cookie, persistent answer storage, or server-side answer processing. Profile, locale, answers, labs, and results are in React memory. Restart, reload, navigation away, or session closure clears that state. The assessment-only leave confirmation protects work in progress; results intentionally clear when left. Evidence links navigate externally only when clicked. Lab OCR/PDF processing remains local; its only parser asset requests are packaged same-origin `/lab-assets/*` files.
