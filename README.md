# Will I Die Soon? — Health Risk Explorer

> **Private research prototype.** This experience is not clinically validated, does not diagnose or predict death, and is not a public wellness or regulated medical release. Do not treat its qualitative outputs or Life's Essential 8 score as medical advice, a disease probability, life expectancy, or a substitute for care.

An English-default, fully bilingual English/French health-risk exploration that keeps the journey in browser memory. A light landing presents a stylized 3D human silhouette surrounded by four health axes; the questionnaire and results report make the questions, points of support, evidence and next actions easier to read.

## Implemented scope

- **157 curated, fully structured questions** across 39 health domains, with stable IDs, deterministic eligibility, age gates, and answer-driven branches. The 2026-09-29 rewrite removed every free-text question and every question that no displayed computation read (58 removals, including the typed `lab_value_*` fields now superseded by the structured lab import), and added 19 structured items required by published screening instruments or by sourced red flags (exertional chest pain, exertional leg pain, irregular palpitations, cancer alarm signs). A second pass the same day removed 78 further context-only questions that no risk rule, screening instrument, Purity Score input, Express component, branch gate or displayed view read, together with the five domains they left empty (dental health, over-the-counter medicines, reproductive health, vaccinations, work exposures). Three conditional questions now cover diabetes type, age at diagnosis and current blood-pressure treatment. Every remaining question feeds at least one displayed output; the social-support, balance-training, morning-light, mood-support and belonging items stay because the protective-roots overview displays them. The bank keeps former-smoking history; the walking-pace, stair, resting-heart-rate, grip, chair-rise and push-up items that only the unrendered longevity module read were removed in the second pass. The presentation separates a short question from useful criteria and explains common questions through their practical purpose. The 2026-10-07 scientific review added the major missing predictor items (cigarettes per day, years smoked and time since quitting for an approximate pack-year total, second-hand smoke at home, walking pace, falls from age 60, medication count and high-risk classes, financial strain, pregnancy complications, menopause before 45, erectile difficulty from 40, diastolic blood pressure, weekly vigorous minutes and the 30-second chair stand) and removed the walking-days, bedtime-variation and minoxidil-context items that no displayed computation read. The same day, the squat and deadlift one-rep-max lifts came back as optional Express strength inputs (read as a load-to-body-weight ratio against a stated product convention, never a mortality predictor), and four items joined for the mortality and lung-cancer models: the four Lee functional difficulties, heart failure behind a declared heart condition, COPD (chronic bronchitis or emphysema) behind a declared lung condition, first-degree family lung cancer and a six-level education follow-up for ever-smokers with ten or more years of schooling.
- **Probable conditions from published instruments:** adult Quick/Detailed/Deep results add a "Conditions" section computed by the pure module `app/lib/pathology-risk.ts` from structured answers, the profile and confirmed lab values. Instruments: FINDRISC (type 2 diabetes), one country-appropriate cardiovascular estimate (SCORE2/SCORE2-OP in four ESC risk regions, SCORE2-Diabetes for eligible type 2 diabetes, US PREVENT-ASCVD, or printed WHO 2019 charts in 21 regions), STOP-Bang (obstructive sleep apnoea), AUDIT-C (hazardous drinking), PHQ-2 (depression), GAD-2 (anxiety), COPD-PS (COPD, age 35+), CAIDE (20-year dementia risk, ages 40–64) and, since `pathology-scores-v4` (2026-10-07), the Lee index (4-year all-cause mortality, age 50+) and PLCOm2012 (6-year lung-cancer risk in ever-smokers aged 50–80). Each card states the status (complete, incomplete with named missing inputs, or not applicable with the reason), category, inputs used (derived inputs flagged) and sources. HbA1c, fasting glucose and eGFR are classified against ADA and KDIGO published ranges, and 14 Lancet 2024 modifiable dementia factors are listed as present, absent or unanswered. Percentages appear under `prototypePolicy`, which every build uses, including the public Vercel alias (owner decision of 2026-10-01); every output is a screening estimate, not a diagnosis. Minors never receive these scores; adult Express results include them since 2026-10-02.
- **Completable estimates (2026-10-02, `pathology-scores-v2`):** published percentages read as "about N in 100" with a decorative 10 × 10 grid. An incomplete card missing at most two declarative answers shows the range of results those answers could still give (never for sex, body size, blood pressure or labs). A complete FINDRISC, CAIDE or SCORE2 card compares the same profile with healthier declared habits (daily activity and vegetables or fruit, 150 weekly minutes, no smoking): a score comparison, not a promise. Each complete card, or range with a settled category, adds one sourced next step. "Complete my estimates" then asks only the missing questions, per card or for all of them, with measurement help for waist, neck and blood pressure and the local lab import; every result is recomputed in place. See [the design record](docs/reviews/2026-10-02-estimations-completables-design.md).
- **Country-based cardiovascular models (`pathology-scores-v3`):** the ESC, US and WHO routes share one card; no missing cholesterol is silently imputed. WHO uses the printed laboratory cell when total cholesterol is confirmed, or a BMI-based non-laboratory cell when diabetes has not been reported; reported diabetes without cholesterol keeps the laboratory estimate incomplete. Region, model variant, screening boundary and bilingual next step are shown and exported without raw input values. WHO cells are reproducible from the official regional PDFs with `scripts/generate-who-cvd-charts.py`; see [the approved model design](docs/reviews/2026-10-03-modeles-cardiovasculaires-par-pays-design.md).
- **Estimated probabilities of major outcomes (`pathology-scores-v4`, 2026-10-07):** the Conditions section opens with a block listing every instrument that publishes a percentage, in a fixed order (death from any cause within four years, cardiovascular disease, lung cancer within six years, type 2 diabetes, dementia), each with its horizon, its status (the percentage, "estimate pending · N questions at most" from the follow-up planner, "blood test needed", or "not applicable to you") and the population it was validated in. Two instruments were added for it. The **Lee index** (Lee et al., JAMA 2006) sums age, sex, diabetes, cancer, lung disease, heart failure, BMI under 25, current smoking and four functional difficulties (bathing, managing finances, walking several blocks, pushing or pulling heavy objects) into 0–26 points and reads the four-year mortality observed by point score in the published validation cohort (1 % at 0 points to 64 % at 14 or more); heart failure and COPD are asked by their own questions only after a heart or blood-vessel condition, or a lung condition, is declared, so asthma alone scores no lung-disease points. **PLCOm2012** (Tammemägi et al., NEJM 2013) applies the published logistic coefficients (age, six-level education, BMI, COPD from the same dedicated question, personal cancer history, first-degree family lung cancer, current smoking, cigarettes per day, years smoked, years since quitting) and reads 1.5 % or more as the screening-selection threshold; never-smokers and ages outside 50–80 are not applicable, the race term stays at its reference category and ages outside the 55–74 cohort are flagged as extrapolated. Both compare quitting smoking at an equal profile. The block's introduction states that it covers only outcomes for which a validated model exists and fits the data, that it is not a list of every cause of death, and that no number is an individual prediction. The follow-up planner now resolves compound branch conditions, counting a hidden input behind the still-open gate of its condition.
- **Four ordered presentation pillars:** Cardio, VO₂ max & cellular energy / *Cardio, VO₂ max et énergie cellulaire*; Strength, nervous system & recovery / *Force, système nerveux et récupération*; Sleep & circadian rhythm / *Sommeil et rythme circadien*; Nutrition & metabolic health / *Alimentation et santé métabolique*. This is presentation grouping only.
- **Closed mapping guards:** tests require every current question (157), domain (39), and risk rule (68) to resolve exactly once; they also check 59 conditional questions, 75 gate edges, and the 22/26/2/18 risk-rule pillar distribution.
- **Express:** exactly 12 adult-only targeted questions, under 1 minute, covering reported VO₂ max, weekly activity minutes, the 30-second chair stand, body weight (asked right before the lifts it scales, and the one Express answer that cannot be skipped), the optional squat and deadlift one-rep maxima, strength days, sleep and nutrition. It returns a four-axis profile, an explicit fitness-and-habits index (`express-index-v2`), up to three priorities, and the reported measurements. Eight interpretable components, plus the two optional lifts when both a load and a body weight are given, feed Cardio, Strength, Sleep and Nutrition: VO₂ max is compared with FRIEND-registry percentiles by age band and sex (US adults 20–79 on treadmill testing; 18–19 and 80+ read the nearest band as an extrapolation) and the chair stand with the Rikli & Jones normative ranges by age and sex (published for community-dwelling adults 60–94, so the component applies only from age 60), while activity, sleep and food items are read against public guidelines. Each optional lift scores the load-to-body-weight ratio against a product convention (1 × body weight for the squat, 1.5 × for the deadlift, capped at 100) with no age or sex norm; the questions ask only for a result already known and say not to attempt a maximal test, and a blank lift neither counts nor blocks completeness. At least four components across two axes are required for a provisional index; an index is complete when every applicable component is answered. Missing measurements stay missing. This separate product index is not a clinical risk estimate or a diagnosis; see [the complete Express method](docs/reviews/2026-09-07-express-profile.md).
- **Quick:** up to 20 eligible core questions, about 3 minutes; adults and adolescents reach the full 20, a guardian-assisted child under 13 currently has 17 eligible items, and the depth needs at least 10. For adults the 20 now cover the complete FINDRISC item set, PHQ-2, the four immediate red flags, blood-pressure and diagnosed-condition context, and the tobacco, alcohol and lab-import gates; adolescents swap waist and glucose history for pregnancy relevance and sexual-contact safety.
- **Detailed (medium-depth):** up to 50 eligible questions, about 8 minutes; adults and adolescents reach the full 50, a child under 13 currently has 41, and the depth needs at least 30. It adds the no-lab instrument items that Quick lacks (STOP-Bang, GAD-2, COPD-PS from age 35, CAIDE education and the SCORE2 prior-event exclusion). The queue keeps the 50 highest-priority eligible questions, with the answer-based Life's Essential 8 inputs ahead of context-only ones; the three COPD-PS items open at 35. The SCORE2 statin, family-history and inflammatory-condition modifiers, the diagnosed-high-cholesterol item, the recreational-substance gates, the dementia history items and the leg-pain and palpitation red flags are asked in Deep only.
- **Deep:** every relevant question. The queue takes all eligible unconditional questions plus the branches that answers open, capped at 200; currently 92 base items at age 18, 94–95 at 35 depending on the sex-specific branches (the COPD-PS items open at 35), 97 at 60 (the Lee and PLCOm2012 items open at 50) and up to 103 with the medication follow-up branch, for 15–20 minutes. Deep is reserved for adults (18+) with at least 80 eligible base questions; minors are offered Detailed instead of a silently shortened queue.
- **English + French:** English is the default. The complete interface, questions, qualitative results, score/action presentation, and exports localize to French without changing canonical IDs, values, units, rules, scores, or state. Locale is not persisted or placed in the URL.
- **Age and country routes:** the profile accepts whole ages 0–120 and a country selected from published ESC, WHO or US coverage, or "other". Children under 13 require an assisting parent/guardian/trusted adult; adolescents 13–17 choose assisted or private answering; adults use the adult route. Age and country still filter question/rule applicability.
- **Local laboratory context:** manual entry is always available. Local import accepts plain-text `.txt`/`.text`, PDF, and image files; CSV and every other unsupported type fall back to manual entry. Extraction uses lazy PDF.js/Tesseract parsers and same-origin vendored worker assets. Draft rows require explicit review. Confirmed rows (`ConfirmedLabValue[]`) are the only numeric lab source: the import no longer writes `lab_value_*` answers. Confirmed total cholesterol feeds ESC and WHO models, HDL feeds ESC and PREVENT, HbA1c and eGFR feed SCORE2-Diabetes, eGFR feeds PREVENT, and total cholesterol feeds CAIDE; classifications use the same confirmed HbA1c/glucose/eGFR values. Unit conversion to canonical mmol/L, mmol/mol and mL/min/1.73 m² is handled in `app/lib/labs.ts`. Limits are 20 MiB, 50 PDF pages, and 500,000 extracted characters; the OCR input/output limits are guardrails, not hard peak-memory bounds for the decoded image and materialized OCR output.
- **Clearer questions and semantic validation:** a known VO₂ max value is explicitly optional, with instructions to use an existing result and not perform a new test visible before the input; the 30-second chair stand carries a stability-and-safety notice. Applicable 0–10 scales have endpoint labels. Click and Enter share validation for missing or non-finite values, nonnegative quantities, positive height/weight, and unit-based bounds such as 0–24 sleep hours and 0–7 activity days per week. Errors are localized and announced; a deliberate skip remains available and stores `null`. These bounds are not medical reference ranges.
- **Explainable results:** immediate signals remain first. Adult Quick/Detailed/Deep reports then show support counts, topics to explore, metric coverage and four descriptive pillar cards, then the "Conditions" screening section, followed by up to three prioritized actions, the published Life's Essential 8 scale reference, the keyboard-accessible risk tree and evidence, the detailed score ledger, confirmed labs and export controls. Counts describe matched signals, not severity or domain scores. Express shows its own qualified profile and four scored axes: sleep and nutrition concerns retain priority even when fitness scores are high. Adult Express results then show the "Conditions" section, with a navigation link. Minors retain their distinct routes and private-results handoff. Anchors only link to available sections, and the normal results title receives focus on arrival.
- **Score references:** an eligible adult Life's Essential 8 score is positioned on the published 0–100 scale, labelled with the AHA bands (under 50 low, 50–79 moderate, 80–100 high cardiovascular health) and, as published cohort context, the US adult mean of about 65. The scale is not a percentile, a population ranking, a diagnosis or a health verdict. The SVG has a text alternative; its HTML legend and ticks remain readable on mobile. Quick and insufficient-coverage results explain why no position is shown. Express instead uses a four-axis compass with a dashed 65/100 product reference ring; missing axes are not drawn as zeroes or joined into a complete shape. This reference is not a population average. See the limits below.
- **No longevity estimate:** the former `app/lib/longevity.ts` heuristic and its `longevity-synthesis` component, unrendered since the atlas redesign, were removed from the codebase on 2026-09-30. Estimated age at death, cause-family weightings and year-gain claims are absent from the product.
- **Life's Essential 8:** the adult score applies the American Heart Association's published 0–100 cut-points to eight metrics (diet, physical activity, nicotine exposure, sleep, body-mass index, non-HDL cholesterol, glycaemic status and blood pressure) and reports their unweighted mean once at least five metrics can be assessed (`essential-eight-v1`). It remains unavailable for Express; the separate Express index does not bypass that gate or change this score. Quick shows a reflection rather than a number; under-18 or unverified-age routes do not receive an adult score. The score describes cardiovascular-health metrics; it is not a diagnosis, a mortality prediction or a personal verdict.
- **Explicit local handoff:** print and redacted JSON require clicks. The report schema is now `health-risk-explorer-report-v6`: adult reports, Express included, add a `pathologyRisk` object (ruleset `pathology-scores-v4`, every instrument with its status, points where applicable, category, internal level, cardiovascular variant and region, input identifiers (derived inputs flagged, answer values never written), missing inputs or not-applicable reason, source IDs, and where available a `range`, a habit `gain` and an `orientation` identifier; plus lab classifications, dementia factors, family-history context, dementia source IDs and the fixed interpretation `published-screening-instruments-not-a-diagnosis`); `riskPercent` and `riskHorizonYears` are present only when the policy allows them. Structured raw answers remain an adult-only opt-in; filenames, raw file/parser content, and private metadata are excluded, and the bank no longer contains free-text answers.

## Evidence limits

The private prototype evaluates 68 transparent qualitative rules across 14 declared groups (12 nonempty). It contains 28 authoritative-safety, 34 guideline-action, and 6 evidence-limited outputs, with **zero validated-estimate rules and zero clinical percentages inside the risk engine**. No hidden model or remote inference is used. Medication/substance outputs do not instruct starting, stopping, or changing a dose.

The separate pathology module (`app/lib/pathology-risk.ts`) is the only place that produces a percentage, and only from published instrument coefficients, cut-offs or printed cells: FINDRISC (Lindström & Tuomilehto 2003), SCORE2/SCORE2-OP (ESC 2021, four regional calibrations), SCORE2-Diabetes (ESC 2023), PREVENT-ASCVD (AHA 2023), WHO 2019 regional cardiovascular charts, CAIDE model 1 (Kivipelto 2006), the Lee index (Lee 2006, validation-cohort mortality by point score), PLCOm2012 (Tammemägi 2013), STOP-Bang, AUDIT-C, PHQ-2, GAD-2 and COPD-PS. Lab bands follow the ADA 2025 Standards of Care and KDIGO 2024, and the dementia factor list follows the 2024 Lancet Commission. These are screening or risk-stratification tools validated in their own cohorts, not a diagnosis or product validation; the "other" country route does not invent a regional calibration. `prototypePolicy.allowValidatedProbabilities` is `true` so percentages display; `publicWellnessPolicy` keeps it `false` and the module then returns categories without probabilities. Every build, including the public Vercel alias, uses `prototypePolicy`: on 2026-10-01 the owner chose to keep the percentages public. `publicWellnessPolicy` stays unwired because, as defined, it would also turn off the urgent-signal interruption and the prompt-review signals. Missing inputs are named rather than imputed, "unsure"/"unknown" answers count as missing, and a single lab value never defines a disease. No instrument is computed for anyone under 18. See the [cardiovascular model design](docs/reviews/2026-10-03-modeles-cardiovasculaires-par-pays-design.md) and the [original screening design](docs/reviews/2026-09-29-pathologies-probables-design.md).

The scale reference consumes the adult Life's Essential 8 score without changing its arithmetic or eligibility. Detailed and Deep show a numeric score once at least five of the eight metrics are assessable; missing metrics reduce the displayed coverage instead of scoring zero, and unanswered behaviour items never lower the score. The band labels (low under 50, moderate 50–79, high 80–100) come from the AHA publication, and the US adult mean of about 65 from its cohort description; both are population-level context, not a percentile or an individual risk estimate. No participant cohort is collected or simulated by the app.

The Express index (`express-index-v2`) is an equally weighted mean of available axes, using unrounded component and axis values until the displayed result is rounded. Its VO₂ max component is the FRIEND-registry percentile for the visitor's age band and sex, averaged with the share of the 150-minute weekly activity guideline; its strength component compares the 30-second chair stand with the Rikli & Jones age- and sex-specific ranges from age 60, counts guideline strengthening days and, when reported with a body weight, adds the optional squat and deadlift ratios against the product convention above. Sleep duration follows the same 0–100 bands as the Life's Essential 8 metric (7–9 hours at 100), a published-screener proxy rather than a clinical calibration. The food-frequency points, signal thresholds and profile levels remain product choices that general guidance does not validate. A complete result can be favorable, mixed or require attention; missing components keep the profile incomplete unless a sleep/nutrition signal takes priority. The [Express method and examples](docs/reviews/2026-09-07-express-profile.md) detail every formula, threshold, missing-data rule and source.

The product computes no life expectancy or age at death. The former longevity helper (`app/lib/longevity.ts`), an unvalidated heuristic based on a simplified life table and year-denominated adjustments, had not been called by the results presentation since the atlas redesign and was deleted on 2026-09-30; the overview, pillar cards, reference curve and export report never used its values.

See [the complete evidence register](docs/evidence-register.md) for every rule, source, population and consumption status, and [the privacy/release record](docs/privacy-and-release.md) for the data boundary, audits, response hardening, and exact private-deployment prerequisites.

## Privacy boundary

There is no account, application backend, database, analytics, session replay, cookie, persistent answer storage, or server-side answer processing. Profile, locale, answers, labs, and results are in React memory. Restart, reload, navigation away, or session closure clears that state. The assessment-only leave confirmation protects work in progress; results intentionally clear when left. Evidence links navigate externally only when clicked. Lab OCR/PDF processing remains local; its only parser asset requests are packaged same-origin `/lab-assets/*` files.
